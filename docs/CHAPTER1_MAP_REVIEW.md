# Chapter 1 Map Review

Chapter 1 consists of four compact Tiled scenes with a deliberate spatial progression. Their terrain routes, landmarks and side spaces are authored around the existing story markers and interaction coordinates.

| Map | Main route | Distinct spaces and landmark composition |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal Camp | Southern spawn → campfire clearing → branching west/east paths | Keeper house and supply tent form a camp compound; dock and boat sit on the west beach; two east exits lead to chapter regions |
| Glowglade Grove | Western entrance → winding forest trail → eastern creek bank | Woodland cottage and field shelter sit in separate clearings; mushrooms and flowers cluster off the trail; the bridge marks the creek crossing |
| Tidal Ruins | Western entrance → stone processional → central shrine | Paired tidal pools frame the stone platform; matching altars flank the shrine; crystal encounter and boss marker sit on the eastern approach |
| Lighthouse Courtyard | Southern entrance → central forecourt → guardian arena | The lighthouse anchors the north; paired columns and broken walls frame the court; garden pockets remain outside the clear boss arena |

## Authoring and runtime rules

- Each map is 18 × 12 cells at 56 px per cell.
- Tiled layers own ground, collision, scene objects and story markers.
- Large buildings, tree trunks, water, cliff bases, boulders and fence segments compile into movement collision.
- Bridge objects explicitly open a walkable span through water.
- Story markers are checked for collision and four-direction reachability from the map spawn.
- Existing story coordinates are preserved: keeper at (7,7), Moss Lumi at (6,8), Moatail at (14,7), Crystal Gecko at (13,8), and authored shrine/boss markers.
- Static terrain and shoreline layers are cached per active area; Raspberry Pi uses bounded weather particles and reduced overdraw.

The route layout is described in `docs/LEVEL_DESIGN.md`. The preview sheet generated by `tools/preview_tiled_maps.py` is the visual QA artifact; it reads the same TMX files as the runtime compiler.
